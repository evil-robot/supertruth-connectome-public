It lists 211,577 reconstructed cells; 166,700 of them are classified neurons, and we used every one. Between those neurons the release records 25.6 million connected pairs. We kept the 6,242,118 joined by five or more synapses, the threshold the FlyWire analyses use to separate reliable connections from ones a single misplaced synapse could create, and we moved none of them. To our knowledge, as of 20 September 2026, this is the first reported use of a whole central nervous system connectome to score the trustworthiness of health data.

**h2: What we held fixed, and what we let move**

The MaleCNS v1.0 connectome was released on 8 June 2026 and described in Cell on 3 September 2026. We exported its neuron-to-neuron connection table, kept every pair joined by five or more synapses, and gave each connection a fixed sign from the sending neuron's predicted neurotransmitter. That left 166,700 neurons and 6,242,118 connections. None of them moved during training.

Three things moved and nothing else: the gain on each connection, which is how loudly it speaks; an input projection that writes a record's fields into 17,937 sensory neurons; and a readout that takes the score from 1,316 descending neurons and 815 motor neurons, the cells a fly uses to act. The wiring decided what could reach what. Training decided how loud. The teacher for the health records was our deployed DTI engine, which scores a record from 0 to 100 across eight dimensions. A second task used VIGIL's Behavioral Integrity Index (BII) on synthetic software-agent event logs, with VIGIL's scorer as the teacher.

**h2: What we tested it against**

A result with no control is a story. We ran five: the same graph with its connections shuffled, each neuron keeping its own number of connections; a random graph at the same density; a conventional network with a matched number of trainable parameters; a linear readout on the raw record fields; and Claude Opus 5, given our published DTI paper and the same records and asked to score them. The shuffle is the control that matters, because it keeps every neuron's connection count and every sign and destroys only who connects to whom, so if the fly's wiring carries no information the shuffled graph does as well as the real one. We wrote the controls and the decision rules before the first run, and we publish the result either way.

**h2: The same record gets the same score, every time**

A judge you can audit has to hold still. Ask the fly's wiring the same question twice and you get the same answer, bit for bit. Ask Claude Opus 5 the same question three times, same prompt, same record, same settings, and you get three answers. On 0 of 300 records did it return the same eight scores twice. The composite moved on 287 of the 300, by as much as 8 points. The tier, the label a hospital would act on, moved on 14. [RESULT: the same three numbers for GPT-5, Grok 4 and Gemini 3 Flash from their runs.] These are measured on 20 September 2026 from SuperTruth's own accounts, priced and logged call by call in the paper.

We say this carefully. Our pre-registered rule lets us claim "more deterministic" only when the model's tier changes on more than 10% of records, and Claude Opus 5 held its tier on 95.3%. So we do not make that claim. We report what we measured, side by side, and let you decide what a judge that changes its mind on the same evidence is worth.
